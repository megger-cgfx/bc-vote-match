# M8 Voice & Messaging — how we talk to people who have been reached by extremist narratives

_Status: draft for human review. Nothing here is published or sent. Owner: eagle (M8 voice workstream)._
_Scope of the M8 outreach wave: see `marketing/BRIEF-FOR-WORKERS.md`. Neutrality rules: see `docs/content/NEUTRALITY-PROTOCOL.md`._

Use this document if you are about to write a comment, a reply, a DM, or a post title aimed at
someone who has been exposed to extremist, conspiratorial, or hyper-partisan narratives around the
BC 2026 election — or around any election. It is the voice half of the M8 strategy; the audience map
(`m8-audience.md`) and the channel plan (`m8-channel-plan.md`) sit beside it.

If you only read one section, read Section 1 and the do/don't table in Section 3. They are enough to
write a compliant comment.

---

## 0. Boundaries (read before anything else)

This guide governs **tone and messaging between people**. It does not license partisan argument.

1. **The Neutrality Protocol still binds.** M8 copy says "here is where you sit", never "party X is
   wrong", "party Y is lying", or any judgement about a party, a candidate, a supporter, or an
   outcome. Nothing in this voice guide overrides that. Speech we are responding to might be about a
   party; our answer does not become partisan to match it.
2. **No manipulation, no impersonation, no astroturfing.** This is a hard line, not a preference.
   Do not pretend to be an ordinary local, a random passer-by, or a member of a group you are not
   in. Do not run multiple accounts. Do not coordinate upvotes, downvotes, or reports. Do not
   pretend to be neutral when you are advancing a product. If the connection matters, disclose it.
3. **These are real people, not targets.** The goal is to keep a door open and lower the temperature,
   not to win a thread. A "win" that humiliates someone closes the door for good.
4. **Drafts only.** Everything produced under M8 is a draft for human review. No M8 text gets posted,
   scheduled, or sent by an agent.
5. **Platform rules first.** Reddit's own rules and each subreddit's rules outrank anything in this
   document. If the two disagree, Reddit wins and you do not post. See Section 5.

## 1. Voice statement

**We sound: curious, calm, plain, level, transparent.**

- **Curious** — we ask before we tell. We are genuinely interested in how the person got to where
  they are, and we treat that as the interesting thing in the conversation.
- **Calm** — no urgency, no alarm, no exclamation marks. Nothing we say is designed to spike
  someone's heart rate. High-heat claims get a low-heat answer.
- **Plain** — short words, short sentences, no jargon, no clinical vocabulary, no marketing voice.
  We write like a person talking across a table.
- **Level** — we take the person seriously without adopting their framing, and we are honest about
  what we do and don't know. We never claim more certainty than we have.
- **Transparent** — we say who we are and what we are doing there. We do not have a secret angle,
  because there isn't one.

### Anti-patterns — what we never sound like

| Never sound like | What it looks like | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Preachy | "Let me educate you." / "The truth is simple." | Lecture = status attack; it triggers defence, not thought. |
| Condescending | "Anyone with a brain can see…" / "You clearly haven't read…" | Insults the person; the claim stops mattering. |
| Clinical | "That's a classic radicalisation pattern." / "You're exhibiting cognitive bias." | Diagnosing a stranger pathologises them and burns the bridge. |
| Alarmist | "This is how democracies die." / "Once it starts it never stops." | Emergency tone confirms the "everything is a crisis" frame and reads as manipulation. |
| "Debate-me" energy | "Debate me on this." / "Source? Cite it." / "I'll wait." | Turns a conversation into a scoring match, which we always lose on optics. |
| Dunking / gotcha | Screenshotting, mock-quoting, "this you?" | Public humiliation. Guarantees the person digs in. |
| Therapist-speak | "How does that make you feel?" used as a lever | Weaponised empathy reads as fake and is a manipulation tactic. |
| Undercover / false-flag | Posting as "just a random voter, no connection to anyone" | Impersonation and misleading participation; Reddit-prohibited. |

One rule of thumb: **if a line would feel bad to receive, it will not work.**

## 2. Messaging principles

Seven principles. Each has at least one concrete line you can use or adapt. Section 3 turns them into
copy-paste pairs.

### P1. Lead with curiosity, not correction.
Open by asking, not by asserting. Your first sentence should leave the person something to answer, not
something to defend against.
> "There's a lot of noise about this one. What was the thing that first made you start looking into it?"

### P2. Ask before you assert.
Until you understand what someone believes and why, you do not know what would actually move it. Get
one honest answer before you offer one fact.
> "Before I say anything — where did you see that? I've been trying to find the original source myself."

### P3. Never dunk. No scores, no gotchas.
No mock-quoting, no "as I already said", no tallying who is winning. Let a point land and stop punch-
ing after it.
> "That's a fair question. I don't have a good answer for the second half, honestly."

### P4. Preserve dignity — target the claim, never the person.
Disagree with a mechanism, a source, or a logic step. Never with someone's intelligence, education,
or character. Assume good faith until it is conclusively absent.
> "I see it differently, and I'd rather understand your take than argue past it. What convinced you?"

### P5. Offer an exit ramp, not a verdict.
The goal is to leave the door open, not to close the case. A verdict ends the conversation; an exit
ramp lets someone come back later without losing face.
> "No pressure to agree — I'm around if you ever want to compare notes on any of this."

### P6. Disclose who you are when it could matter.
If you are connected to BC Vote Match, say so in the first comment you make in a thread, before it
comes up. Hidden affiliation reads as astroturfing the moment it surfaces, and it undoes every good
line above it.
> "Disclosure: I help build a BC voter-information tool (not affiliated with any party). This is just
> conversation; ignore the tool if it's not your thing."

### P7. Match the room and earn the right to speak.
Read the subreddit rules and a week of the community before you post. Contribute to a thread as a
member of the room, not as an envoy to it. If the community does not want this topic, you do not raise
it.
> "Long-time reader here. Not trying to change anyone's mind — just want to understand the argument."

### P8. Leave while it is still going well.
When a thread heats up, gets hostile, or starts repeating, stop replying. Disengagement is a voice
choice, not a failure; the best de-escalation is often the comment you don't post.
> "I'll leave it there so it doesn't turn into a thing. Genuine thanks for the back-and-forth."

## 3. Do / Don't table (copy from here)

Bad phrasing (left) -> rewritten phrasing (right). The right column is safe to adapt.

| # | Don't write | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "You've been lied to by [party]'s propaganda. Here's the actual truth." | "There's a lot of conflicting stuff out there on this. What's the part that made you start looking into it?" |
| 2 | "Source? That's obviously fake." | "Where did you see that? I've been trying to track down the original myself." |
| 3 | "Anyone with a brain can see [claim] is nonsense." | "I don't know how to check the whole system — but I can check one claim with you. Want to try?" |
| 4 | "Let me educate you on how this actually works." | "Want to compare notes? I'll walk you through how I check a claim like this." |
| 5 | "That's an insane take." | "I see it differently, and I'd rather understand where you're coming from." |
| 6 | "You're either misinformed or trolling." | "I think we're working from different information. What convinced you of your side?" |
| 7 | "Have you tried just reading actual news?" | "Here's the one source I find most useful — what do you read that you trust?" |
| 8 | "I'm just a random local like you, no connection to anyone." | "Disclosure: I help build a BC voter tool. Not affiliated with any party. Happy to just talk." |
| 9 | "Nobody here is buying this." (everyone pile on) | Don't post. Or: "I'll leave it there — happy to keep comparing notes whenever." |
| 10 | "The science says you're wrong, and that's that." | "I could be missing something. What would change your mind about this?" |

One more "don't" that isn't a rewrite: parting insults ("cry more", "touch grass", any closing jab).
The answer is not a better line — it is to stop replying (P8).

## 4. Tone adjustments per context

Same voice, different dial. The core (curious, calm, plain, level, transparent) never changes.

### Top-level comment (in a thread we did not start)
- **Length:** 2–4 sentences. The point is to open, not to land a case.
- **Opening:** ask one genuine question or offer one honest observation. No links in the first comment
  you make in a new community.
- **Affiliation:** disclose at the end if relevant, once. Do not repeat it in every reply.
- **Goal:** get one reply. That is the whole success condition.
- Example: "Reading through this thread, the thing I can't square is [specific detail]. What am I
  missing? (I help build a BC voter tool, no party ties — here to understand, not to argue.)"

### Reply (someone answered us, or we are answering a specific comment)
- **Length:** 1–3 sentences. Match their length; long replies to short comments read as lecturing.
- **Move:** reflect their point back first ("So the worry is…"), then add at most one thing. Ask before
  you assert (P2).
- **Disagreement:** target the claim, never the person (P4). Never say "you're wrong"; say "I see it
  differently."
- **When it heats up:** stop. One reply of de-escalation, then silence beats a third round.
- Example: "That's a fair worry, and it's not one I can wave away. The bit I keep getting stuck on is
  [specific]. How do you get past that?"

### DM (direct message)
- **Default: do not open an unsolicited DM.** Reddit prohibits sending large amounts of unsolicited
  private messages, and most communities treat cold DMs as harassment. See Section 5.
- **Only DM when:** the person moved the conversation to DMs themselves, the person explicitly invited
  a DM, or you are answering a mod/organiser who contacted you first.
- **Length:** 1–3 sentences, no links in the first message.
- **Disclose up front** (P6). No secrets in DMs.
- **Never:** open with a fact-check, open with a link, copy-paste the same DM to several people, or
  send anything that would not survive being screenshotted.
- **Crisis:** if someone describes self-harm or immediate danger, do not counsel them. Send one short
  message pointing to a real local crisis line or the appropriate subreddit resource, then stop. (Handled
  in detail by the safety review, `m8-safety-review.md`.)

### Post title
- **Never a question that is secretly an argument** ("Isn't it obvious that…?").
- **No alarm, no accusation, no party names.** Descriptive and neutral, or don't post.
- **Short and literal.** Title states the topic; the body carries the stakes.
- Good: "How do you verify a claim like this? Genuine question from a non-expert."
- Bad: "Why is nobody talking about what [party] is really doing?"

## 5. Reddit norms and platform rules (non-negotiable)

These are Reddit's rules, not ours. Cited from Reddit's published policies (read 2026-10-01); re-check
the current wording at `redditinc.com/policies/reddit-rules` before posting.

- **"Remember the human."** No harassment, bullying, or attacks on a person or a group.
- **"Be authentic."** Reddit states you need not use your real name, but you must not "intentionally
  mislead others or impersonate an individual or entity in a deceptive manner." That covers posting as
  an unconnected local when you are not.
- **"Participate authentically in communities where you have a personal interest, and do not spam or
  engage in disruptive behaviors (including content manipulation)."** Coordinated posting across
  accounts or communities fails this test.
- **Vote manipulation is prohibited:** multiple accounts, coordinated voting, automation to move vote
  counts, ban evasion. We never ask for upvotes, never coordinate votes, never run more than one
  account per person.
- **Spam includes "repeated or unsolicited mass engagement"** and "sending large amounts of
  unsolicited chat or private messages." No mass DMs, no reposted identical comments, no dropping the
  same link community after community.
- **Community rules outrank us.** Each subreddit sets its own rules; moderators decide what counts as
  off-topic or unwanted. Read the sidebar/rules before posting and follow them even where they are
  stricter than the sitewide rules.

Practical consequence for M8: one account per person, real disclosure where relevant, no vote
coordination, no cold DMs, no copy-paste, no link-dropping, and lurk-plus-read before you ever post.

## 6. Putting it together — a compliant comment, written from this doc alone

Worked example a fresh poster can copy the shape of. Topic is a generic election-integrity claim; no
party is named and no party is judged.

> **Top-level comment:** "I keep going back and forth on this one, so genuine question: what was the
> first thing that made you doubt the count? I'm asking because I can never tell which parts are
> checkable and which aren't. (Disclosure: I help build a BC voter tool, no ties to any party — here to
> understand, not to argue.)"

> **Reply to a pushback:** "That's fair — 'just trust it' isn't an answer, you're right. I'm not sure I
> can verify the whole system either. The one thing I can do is walk through how I'd check a single
> claim, if that's useful. Or we can leave it — your call."

Both follow every principle: curiosity first (P1), ask before asserting (P2), no dunk (P3), claim not
person (P4), exit ramp and autonomy (P5), disclosed (P6), reads like a room member (P7), and the
poster stops rather than escalating (P8).

## 7. Pre-post self-check (five lines, run every time)

1. Did I ask something before I asserted something? (P1, P2)
2. Is any line aimed at the person rather than the claim? If yes, cut it. (P3, P4)
3. Does this leave the other person somewhere to go, or does it end with a verdict? (P5)
4. Have I disclosed my connection to the tool where it could matter? (P6)
5. Would this survive being screenshotted, and does it follow the subreddit's rules? (P7, Section 5)

If any answer is no, rewrite or don't post.

## 8. Open questions / handoff

- **Neutrality interface.** Working an anti-extremist angle while staying party-neutral is the main risk
  in this document. Section 0 states the rule; the safety review (`m8-safety-review.md`) should
  confirm no M8 line reads as partisan before anything is used.
- **Disclosure wording.** A single, approved disclosure sentence should be fixed by the human owner so
  every poster uses the same one. Draft is in P6; needs sign-off.
- **Evidence base.** The principles draw on motivational-interviewing approaches to disengagement
  (e.g. the *Journal for Deradicalization* article "Motivational Interviewing for Deradicalization",
  journals.sfu.ca/jd) and the general MI literature (e.g. PMC3330017). Secondary/popular sources were
  not treated as authoritative here.
- **Crisis handling** belongs to the safety review, not this document; this doc only says "point to a
  real resource and stop."
