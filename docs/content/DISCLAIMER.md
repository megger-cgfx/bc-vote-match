# Disclaimer

BC Vote Match is an educational tool. Please read this before you use it.

## It is not a how-to-vote guide

This tool does not tell you how to vote, who to vote for, or whether to vote at all. It shows how your answers to 18 policy statements compare with the published positions of the parties. What you do with that comparison is entirely your decision.

We do not endorse parties or candidates. We do not rank parties. We do not publish "best match" language anywhere in the tool or in our outreach.

## It does not predict your vote

A match score is a distance between two sets of policy answers, nothing more. Elections are decided by much more than policy positions: local candidates, party leaders, records in government, competence, trust, strategic considerations, and issues the 18 statements do not cover. Many people will find their closest match is a party they have no intention of voting for. That is a normal result, not an error.

The scores are not polls, forecasts, or predictions of any election outcome.

## It is not the parties speaking

The party codes in this tool are our reading of what the parties have published, drawn from verbatim quotes we archived and linked. The parties did not write, review, approve, or endorse this tool. A party may disagree with how we interpreted its statements. Where that happens, we evaluate the evidence and publish corrections and coding changes in the open.

## Limits of the data

- 18 statements are a sample of BC politics, not a survey of it.
- Positions are coded from published material up to the date shown in the coding table. Parties change positions during campaigns; the tool is versioned and dated, and older versions may be out of date.
- "No position" codes mean we found no statement. They do not mean the party has no view.
- Riding-level views, where shown, use the party's coded positions, not the individual candidate's. Candidates may personally differ from their party.
