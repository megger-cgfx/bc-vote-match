# BC Vote Match — Recommended 18 for v1

Drawn from `questions.json` (54 candidates, 9 per topic). Three per topic, balanced
across the economic/social dimensions (13 of the 18 carry an economic dimension,
8 carry a social dimension, 3 carry both). Every pick is a statement where at least
two of the five parties plausibly diverge, not a valence issue.

## cost-of-living-taxes
- **q01** — reinstate and raise the consumer carbon tax. Sharpest real-world split in the campaign: NDP repealed it, Greens want carbon pricing back, Conservatives/OneBC reject it.
- **q03** — wealth tax on the highest earners. Clean left-right axis: NDP/Greens open to it, Conservatives/OneBC opposed, CentreBC likely hedging.
- **q04** — balance the budget by cutting spending. Fiscal axis that separates Conservatives/OneBC from NDP/Greens and tests CentreBC's positioning.

## housing
- **q10** — override municipal zoning for density near transit. Separates provincial interventionists (NDP, partly Greens/Conservatives on supply) from municipal-autonomy voices (OneBC, localist CentreBC).
- **q11** — strengthen rent controls. Classic splitter: NDP/Greens for, Conservatives/OneBC against, CentreBC in between.
- **q18** — expand supportive/social housing over neighbourhood objections. Adds the social dimension and separates yimby-style supply politics from localist resistance.

## health
- **q19** — expand private for-profit clinics. The defining health-care divide: Conservatives/OneBC favour it, NDP/Greens oppose, CentreBC ambiguous.
- **q22** — fund supervised consumption and safer supply. Sharp social split on the overdose response; mirrors the public-safety cluster from the health-system side.
- **q24** — public dental care. Spending-priority splitter with real fiscal weight; NDP/Greens favour, Conservatives/OneBC sceptical.

## climate-environment
- **q28** — approve new LNG projects. Dominant BC climate-economy divide: Conservatives/OneBC strongly for, Greens against, NDP/CentreBC middle.
- **q29** — stop logging old-growth. Social/environmental axis that separates Greens (and partly NDP) from resource-development parties.
- **q31** — transit and rail over highway expansion. Infrastructure-priority split, Greens vs Conservatives/OneBC, with NDP somewhere in between.

## indigenous-reconciliation
- **q38** — amend DRIPA to rule out Indigenous veto over resource projects. The clearest reconciliation divide: Conservatives/OneBC push it, NDP/Greens resist, CentreBC unclear.
- **q39** — share resource revenue with First Nations. Economic reconciliation measure; distinguishes parties that back revenue sharing from those that frame it as a cost.
- **q43** — prefer Indigenous-owned businesses in public procurement. Splits on preferential policy vs formal-equality framing; adds an economic dimension to the topic.

## public-safety
- **q46** — make public drug use illegal, no return to decriminalization. Top-of-mind issue in the 2026 campaign; Conservatives/OneBC strongly for, Greens opposed, NDP shifted.
- **q47** — tougher bail for repeat violent offenders. Broad-but-not-universal support; distinguishes law-and-order emphasis from civil-liberties caution.
- **q48** — expand involuntary care for severe addiction and mental illness. Emerging cross-partisan-but-contested axis, especially between Greens and everyone else.

## What was cut and why
- **Mirror duplicates**: q15 (municipal final say) is the inverse of q10; q23/q27 duplicate q19's private-care axis; q42 duplicates q38's land-use axis; q52 duplicates q46. Keep one direction per axis so respondents are not asked the same question twice.
- **Consensus/valence items**: q20 (foreign-trained health workers), q41 (Indigenous child services), q45 (languages funding), q51 (treatment beds), q54 (youth programs) — most parties agree in principle, so they score little differentiation.
- **Overlapping substance**: q05 (price caps) and q06 (minimum wage indexing) are narrower versions of the cost-of-living axis already covered by q03/q08; q25 overlaps q22; q50 overlaps q22/q46; q34 (EV mandate) is a single-policy niche.
- **Load-bearing wording risks**: q14 (protect property values) and q02 ("even if smaller public services") are close to leading and were left out of v1 despite decent divergence.
- **Dimension balance**: q08, q12, q13, q16, q17, q33, q36, q40, q44, q49, q53 were good differentiators but lost to the per-topic cap of three.

## Hard-to-differentiate topics
- **indigenous-reconciliation**: all five parties affirm reconciliation in general; differentiation only exists on the specific veto/land-use/procurement wording, so the three picks above deliberately target those fault lines. If coding shows no spread on q43, swap in q44 (accelerated treaties).
- **health**: human-resources and system-administration statements (q20, q26) are close to consensus; the private-care and drug-policy axes are the only reliably separating ones.
